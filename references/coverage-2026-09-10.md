Verdict on this pair: the findings and the tables are carried. Two things are missing.
First, two numeric details: the core-feature selection thresholds of Appendix D and the
extractor validation kappa of Table 7. Second, and this is the one Eren pointed at, the
paper's second instrument. The paper has two instruments: the structured template (Figure 8),
which this skill carries in full, and the feature-space classifier with SHAP importance and
per-story rarity, which this skill only cites. No local tool computes a feature vector, an
importance, or a rarity percentile for a text. The audit therefore runs the template and the
ten checks by reading, and never produces the second instrument's numbers.

Update 2026-09-10, later the same day: the second instrument now exists locally as ONE
merged tool, C:/ErenAI/ARACLAR/storyscope_siniflandirici.py - the classifier layer, the
rarity layer (--reference / --rarity-percentile), and the audit pass folded into it
(--audit: non-fiction default drops the 12 fiction-only columns, direct reader address
stays out unless --third-person per SKILL.md check 9, the three near-tied rows carry WEAK,
output is a 0-100 narrative-AI index). This is the only StoryScope tool path these skills
reference; the separate audit scorer built at 17:46 the same day was folded into it and
archived at C:/ErenAI/_ARSIV/storyscope_narratif_skor.py.

What building it would require, measured from the paper: a feature extractor for the 30 core
features (the paper used Gemini 3 Flash over 304 features, page 5), a labelled pool to train
on (the paper's 52,434 stories, page 5; its human corpus is Books3 and is not released, page 3
footnote 4), and a reference distribution to compute rarity against (train and validation
narrative space, page 22). For our formats, messages and captions of under 300 words, the
paper itself says shorter texts cannot support fine-grained narrative features (page 3,
"enabling extraction of fine-grained narrative features that shorter texts cannot support").
So the honest statement is: a scaled copy of the classifier is not buildable here, and would
not apply to our lengths; a small local version that scores the 30 core features by reading
and reports their spread is buildable, and that is what the ten checks already do by hand.
